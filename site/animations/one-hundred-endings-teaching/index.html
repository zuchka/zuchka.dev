Next-token sampling · 0:48

One Hundred Endings

Lower temperatures favor the likeliest next token. Higher temperatures spread the odds more evenly.

Transcript and sources

Visual transcript

0:00–0:11 The camera starts close on “amber,” then pulls back to reveal a sheet of 100 endings for “The light turned…”. Each spot is one try.

0:11–0:17 At temperature 0.4, the model favors “green.” In this four-word example, “amber” has about a 1% chance per try. It appears three times.

0:17–0:25 What happens at 1.6? There are five seconds to guess. A green slider moves right, and the number on the sheet rises from 0.4 to 1.6. The same 100 draws replay in their original positions.

0:25–0:36 “Amber” appears 15 times. Its chance per try rose to about 16%. “Green” is still the favorite. The footer compares both samples side by side.

0:36–0:48 AI engineers can lower temperature to tag similar refund requests with the same label, “billing.” They can raise it to draft several ways to reply to the same request.

Source

The film uses four illustrative word scores and the same 100 seeded draws at both temperatures. Temperature rescales the scores before sampling. Hugging Face Transformers — temperature in text generation

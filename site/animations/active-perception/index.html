Active perception · 0:32

Where’s the handle?

The mug hides its handle from the front. Moving the camera sideways reveals where to grip. Choosing what to sense next to get the information a task needs is active perception.

Transcript and sources

Visual transcript

0:00–0:05 How can the robot find the handle? The camera pulls back from a field of dots to reveal a mug. Each dot marks a surface point the robot’s camera can see.

0:05–0:11 Would zooming in reveal the handle? The same front image gets bigger. In the top view, the camera stays still. The mug still blocks the handle.

0:11–0:17 What if the camera moves sideways? The camera travels around the stationary mug. The opening in the handle comes into view.

0:17–0:22 Now where can it grip? The front and side observations appear together at the same scale. One gripper finger fits through the opening. The other closes on the outside.

0:22–0:27 Which move helped the robot learn? Moving the camera revealed the grip point. The robot uses that information to lift the mug.

0:27–0:32 Roboticists call this active perception. The front and side observations stay on screen together. The robot chooses what to sense next based on the information it needs.

Sources

Ruzena Bajcsy describes how a system directs its sensing according to the task and what it already knows. Active Perception, Proceedings of the IEEE (1988)

Michel Breyer and colleagues demonstrate a robot choosing additional camera views before grasping a target. The mug in this film illustrates that principle. Closed-Loop Next-Best-View Planning for Target-Driven Grasping, IROS (2022)

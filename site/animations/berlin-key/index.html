Mechanics · 1:14

The Berlin key

In night operation, the key stays captive after unlocking. Push it through, close the door and lock it from the other side to get it back.

Transcript and sources

Visual transcript

0:00–0:08 Two toothed bits share one shaft. A green band marks the end that enters the lock from the street.

0:08–0:21 On the first turn, the center tooth lifts its lever, then the outer pair touch, then the shorter pair. The five openings line up around the pin fixed to the bolt.

0:21–0:31 The bit enters a notch under the bolt and presses its left wall. The bolt slides out of the strike, carrying its pin through the openings.

0:31–0:39 Pulling back brings the bit’s flat face against the upper street rib. Pushing forward sends the groove underneath the bit over the lower courtyard rib.

0:39–0:49 The green band travels to the courtyard. At the other end of the shaft, an upper groove lets the trailing bit slide past the street rib. The key stays in the door.

0:49–0:57 The resident crosses the threshold. Closing the door lets a magnet lift the catch that blocks the bolt on this lock.

0:57–1:14 The second bit takes up the clearance and drives the opposite wall of the notch. The bolt seats in the strike, the key turns upright, and the resident withdraws it on the courtyard side.
